How to Evaluate Whether an Automation Template Is Worth Buying

Your feed is full of "$19 Notion template" and "automation playbook" ads. Some will save you a weekend of setup work. Others are a folder of generic to-do lists with a nice cover image. Here's how to tell the difference before you pay.

By The Automation Edge Team

Small business owners are drowning in pre-built templates right now — Notion dashboards, Zapier/Make blueprints, "automation playbooks," swipe files, you name it. The pitch is always the same: skip the setup, get the system, save hours. Sometimes that's exactly what happens. Other times you pay $19-$49 for a half-finished spreadsheet that needed more editing than building it from scratch would have taken. Here's a practical checklist for telling which is which before you click buy.

1. Does It Solve a Process, or Just Hold Information?

This is the single biggest tell. A genuinely useful template automates a decision or a trigger — it tells you what's overdue, what needs a follow-up, what's about to expire. A weak template is just a prettier container for typing things in by hand.

  • Good sign: It includes filtered views, status fields, or formulas that surface what needs attention today — not just a place to list everything.
  • Red flag: Every field is free text, there's no status/date logic anywhere, and the "automation" is really just a checklist with checkboxes.

Before buying, ask the seller (or check the preview/demo) one question: "What does this do automatically that I'd otherwise do manually?" If the honest answer is "nothing, you still fill it in yourself," you're paying for formatting, not automation.

2. Can You See a Real Preview — Not Just Screenshots?

Static screenshots hide the two things that matter most: whether the formulas actually work, and whether the relationships between tables/sheets are set up correctly. Look for:

  • A live, click-through demo (a public Notion page, a Google Sheet in view-only mode, a short screen recording) rather than still images.
  • Visible formulas or automations — if it's a spreadsheet, can you see at least one formula bar? If it's a Notion template, are relations and rollups visible in the preview?
  • A clear list of what's included — number of databases/tabs, whether it needs a paid plan of the host tool (e.g., Notion free vs. paid, Airtable base limits) to actually function.

If a seller won't show you anything beyond a polished thumbnail, that's usually because the thumbnail is the best part.

3. Does It Fit Your Tools, or Does It Require New Ones?

A template that assumes you already use Notion, Google Sheets, or Zapier is low-risk — you're just filling a structure you already understand. A template that requires signing up for a brand-new tool, paying for a premium tier you didn't need before, or learning an unfamiliar interface adds real cost on top of the sticker price.

Before buying, map the requirements:

QuestionWhy it matters
Does it need a paid plan I don't already have?Adds a recurring cost on top of the one-time price
Does it need a browser extension, API key, or third-party connector?Extra setup time and ongoing maintenance risk
Is it built for the exact tool I already use (Notion vs. ClickUp vs. Airtable)?Porting between tools often takes longer than building from scratch

This is the same filtering question we walk through in our guide to picking automation tools for freelancers — the best template is the one that fits the stack you already have, not the fanciest one on the market.

4. Do the Reviews (or Lack of Them) Tell You Anything?

Templates sold through Gumroad, Etsy, or a creator's own site often show review counts and ratings — use them, but read a few of the actual comments rather than trusting the star average alone. Look specifically for:

  • Comments mentioning actual use ("I've used this for 3 months and it's saved me X hours") rather than just "nice design."
  • Any mention of broken formulas, missing instructions, or confusing setup — a handful of these across dozens of reviews is normal; a pattern of them is a warning.
  • Whether the seller responds to support questions in the reviews or comments — this tells you what happens if you get stuck after buying.

No reviews at all isn't automatically disqualifying for a brand-new product, but combined with no live preview, it's a combination worth being cautious about.

5. What Does Setup Actually Look Like After You Buy?

The real cost of a template isn't the $19 — it's the time to make it match your business. A good template comes with:

  1. A short setup guide (even a one-page PDF or a linked video) showing exactly how to duplicate and connect the pieces.
  2. Sample data already filled in so you can see the system working before you replace it with your own data.
  3. A realistic time estimate — "15 minutes to duplicate and customize" is a fair claim for a template; "fully automated in 2 minutes" for anything with more than one moving part usually isn't.

If a product page promises zero setup time for something that connects multiple tools or tracks multiple workflows, read that as marketing, not a feature spec.

6. A Quick Gut-Check Before You Buy

Run any template through these five questions before paying for it:

  • Does it automate a decision (what's overdue, what's next) or just store information?
  • Can I see it actually working before I pay — not just a screenshot?
  • Does it work with tools I already have, or does it add new recurring costs?
  • Do real users confirm it works as advertised?
  • Is there a clear setup guide, or am I on my own after I pay?

If you can answer "yes" to at least four of those, it's very likely worth the price of a cheap lunch. If you're answering "no" or "not sure" to most of them, keep looking — or build it yourself using a guide like our walkthrough for a Notion client management system.

Bringing It Together

A $19 template is cheap to buy and expensive to waste time on if it doesn't actually do anything. Before you buy the next one in your feed, run it through the checklist above: does it automate a real decision, can you see it working, does it fit your existing tools, do real reviews back it up, and is setup clearly explained? Four or five "yes" answers means it's worth it.
