Beginner Automation Tools for Social Media & Invoicing

Two of the most time-consuming recurring tasks — social media and billing follow-ups — can be almost entirely automated with simple, beginner-friendly tools you probably already have.

If you run a small business, you already know the feeling: the same tasks come around every single week, and they eat hours you don't have. The trick to reclaiming that time isn't working faster — it's figuring out which repetitive tasks can be automated, and setting up a system once so you never have to think about them again.

Here's how to automate repetitive tasks in a small business using two of the most common time-drains: social media posting and invoice/payment reminders.

1. Batch Social Media Content with AI

Posting consistently on social media is one of those tasks that feels small each day but adds up to hours every week — especially when you're staring at a blank screen trying to think of what to say. The fix is to stop creating content daily and instead batch it once a week using AI to do the heavy lifting.

The 30-minute weekly workflow

  1. Once a week (say, Monday morning), open ChatGPT and give it context: your business, your voice, and 3-5 topics or promotions for the week
  2. Ask it to draft 5-7 captions in your brand voice, with variations for each platform (shorter for Instagram/X, more detail for LinkedIn or Facebook)
  3. Skim and lightly edit the captions — swap in specific details, prices, or a personal anecdote so it doesn't sound generic
  4. Paste the finalized captions into Buffer or Later, attach photos, and schedule them across the week

Example: A local bakery

A bakery owner used to post sporadically, whenever she remembered between orders. Now, every Monday she spends 30 minutes feeding ChatGPT her weekly specials and any events, gets back seven ready-to-edit captions, and schedules the whole week in Buffer in one sitting. Posting went from "maybe twice a week" to a consistent daily presence, and she got that time back for actually baking.

Example: A B2B consulting firm

A two-person consulting shop uses the same approach for LinkedIn: one prompt session generates a week of posts about industry insights, client wins (anonymized), and tips. Later handles the scheduling. What used to be an ad-hoc, easily-skipped task is now a locked-in 30-minute ritual with a full week of content to show for it.

Tips for better AI-drafted captions

  • Feed the AI a few of your best past posts so it can match your tone
  • Always ask for multiple options per topic, then pick and mix the best lines
  • Keep a running doc of recurring themes (weekly specials, testimonials, behind-the-scenes) to speed up prompting even further

2. Automate Invoice and Payment Reminders

Chasing late payments is uncomfortable and time-consuming — and it's exactly the kind of task that should never require a human to remember to do it. Connecting your invoicing tool to an automated reminder workflow solves this permanently.

How to set it up

Whether you use QuickBooks, Wave, or Stripe Invoicing, the process is similar:

  1. Connect your invoicing tool to Zapier or Make (most have native integrations)
  2. Set triggers based on invoice due dates: e.g., 3 days before due, on the due date, and 7 days after if unpaid
  3. At each trigger, have an AI step draft a short, friendly reminder email — polite and low-pressure for the first one, slightly firmer by the third
  4. Auto-send the email, or route it to your inbox for a quick approve-and-send if you prefer a human check first

Example: A freelance design studio

A three-person design studio used to manually track which clients hadn't paid, often noticing weeks late. After connecting Wave to Zapier, invoices now automatically trigger a friendly reminder 3 days before the due date, and if still unpaid, a firmer AI-drafted follow-up 7 days after. Average days-to-payment dropped noticeably, and nobody has to manually track a spreadsheet of who owes what.

Example: A home services contractor

A contractor using QuickBooks connected it to Make so that every invoice over 30 days past due automatically generates an AI-drafted reminder referencing the specific job and amount, sent from the contractor's own email address so it still feels personal. What used to be an awkward monthly phone-call task is now handled quietly in the background.

Why this works better than manual follow-up

Automated reminders are consistent (they never get forgotten), timely (sent exactly when they should be), and remove the emotional friction of asking for money yourself — while the AI-drafted language keeps them from sounding like a form letter.

Putting It Together

Both of these automations share the same pattern: pick a recurring task, connect the right tools with Zapier or Make, and let AI handle the personalized, judgment-heavy part (writing) while automation handles the timing and delivery. Start with whichever task currently annoys you the most — social media or billing — and you'll likely reclaim several hours a month.
