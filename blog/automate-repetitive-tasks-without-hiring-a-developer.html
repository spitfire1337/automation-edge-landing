Zapier vs. Make: Choosing a No-Code Tool to Automate Repetitive Tasks Without a Developer

A tool-selection guide — not just a framework — for finding, building, and maintaining automations without ever hiring a developer.

"We should automate that" is one of the most common sentences said in small businesses — and one of the least acted on. The usual assumption is that automation means hiring a developer, paying for custom software, or learning to code. None of that is true anymore. Modern no-code tools let you automate repetitive tasks in a small business in an afternoon, using nothing more than a web browser and the apps you already use.

Here's exactly how to do it, step by step.

1. Find Your Highest-Value Automation Candidates

Not every repetitive task is worth automating first. Spend one day writing down every task you or your team does more than once a week, then score each one on two things: how often it happens, and how annoying or error-prone it is. The tasks that score high on both are your starting points — usually things like data entry, follow-up emails, appointment scheduling, or invoice creation.

Quick test

Ask yourself: "If I described this task to someone with zero context, could they follow clear, repeatable steps every time?" If yes, it's almost certainly automatable.

2. Zapier vs. Make: Which No-Code Tool to Pick

You don't need to compare dozens of platforms. Two tools cover the vast majority of small business needs:

  • Zapier — best for connecting popular business apps (Gmail, Shopify, QuickBooks, Google Sheets) with simple "if this happens, do that" logic. Great for beginners.
  • Make (formerly Integromat) — slightly more powerful visual workflows for when you need branching logic or multiple steps. Still no code required.

Both have free tiers that are more than enough to automate your first few workflows.

3. Build Your First Workflow in Three Steps

  1. Trigger — the event that starts the automation (a new form submission, a new row in a spreadsheet, a new sale)
  2. Action(s) — what should happen automatically (send an email, create a task, update a record, post a message)
  3. AI step (optional but powerful) — add an AI action to write personalized text, summarize information, or categorize incoming requests, instead of relying on rigid templates

Most first workflows take 15–30 minutes to build and test, and run forever afterward with no maintenance.

4. Real Examples That Replace a Developer

Customer support triage: Incoming support emails are automatically read by an AI step, categorized by urgency and topic, and routed to the right person or folder — a task that used to require a custom ticketing system.

Order fulfillment updates: When an order ships, a Zap automatically pulls the tracking number and sends a personalized "your order is on its way" email — no developer-built integration needed.

Employee onboarding: A new hire added to a spreadsheet automatically triggers welcome emails, account creation reminders, and a checklist assigned to their manager — all without custom HR software.

5. Maintain and Improve Without Technical Help

Because these tools are visual and built for non-developers, you can open any workflow later and adjust it yourself — change an email template, add a new condition, or connect one more app — all through drag-and-drop interfaces. There's no codebase to hand off, no developer to re-hire, and no risk of the automation breaking because "the person who built it left."
